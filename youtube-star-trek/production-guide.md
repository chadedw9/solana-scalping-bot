# Production Guide

How to actually make the episodes — gear, software, the look, SEO, and a
workflow that doesn't burn you out.

## 1. Gear (start cheap, the audio matters most)

You do **not** need a fancy camera. For commentary/lore channels, audio and
visuals-on-screen matter far more than your face.

| Priority | Item | Budget option | Why |
|----------|------|---------------|-----|
| 🔴 #1 | **Microphone** | USB mic (Fifine, Samson Q2U, ~$50–70) | Bad audio makes people leave instantly. This is the #1 upgrade. |
| 🟡 #2 | **Screen recorder / editor** | OBS (free) + DaVinci Resolve (free) | Record voiceover + screen, edit it all. |
| 🟢 #3 | Camera (optional) | Your phone or webcam | Only if you want a talking-head insert. Many big lore channels show NO face. |
| 🟢 #4 | Quiet room + soft furnishings | free | Kills echo. A closet with clothes = a vocal booth. |

**Faceless is fine.** Plenty of huge Trek channels are voice-over-only with
visuals on screen. This lowers the barrier and keeps focus on content.

## 2. Software stack

- **Recording voiceover:** Audacity (free) or OBS (free)
- **Editing:** DaVinci Resolve (free, pro-grade) or CapCut (free, easy)
- **Thumbnails:** Photopea (free Photoshop clone) or Canva — plus the **AI image
  generator** in this workspace for backgrounds/elements
- **B-roll/visuals:** the **AI image + video tools** in this workspace (see
  `tools-and-automation.md`) so you avoid copyrighted clips
- **Captions:** YouTube auto-captions, then clean them up (boosts retention +
  accessibility + SEO)

## 3. The "look" (packaging = consistency)

Pick these once and reuse forever so every video is recognizably *yours*:

- **Intro sting:** 2–3 seconds, your logo + a sound. Never longer.
- **Color palette:** LCARS-inspired (Trek's UI) is iconic — oranges, blues,
  purples on black — but make it *yours*, don't copy the exact UI.
- **Font:** One clean display font for titles, one readable font for on-screen text.
- **Lower-thirds / text style:** Same everywhere.
- **Sign-off line:** Same words every episode (brand recall).

## 4. Thumbnails & titles (80% of your success)

This is where new channels live or die. For every video:

1. Write the **title first** — it shapes the video. Use proven patterns:
   *Explained, Why X, X Ranked, The Truth About X, How X Actually Works.*
2. Make **3 thumbnail variations.** Different text, different focal image.
3. Thumbnail rules:
   - **3 words max** of big, bold text (readable on a phone)
   - One clear focal subject (a ship, a face, an object)
   - High contrast, bright edges, emotion or curiosity
   - Don't restate the title — *complement* it
4. Use the AI image tool to generate or composite backgrounds and elements
   (e.g., a dramatic starship silhouette) without grabbing copyrighted stills.

## 5. SEO / discovery

- **Title:** Front-load the keyword. "Federation No Money, Explained" not "A
  Little Chat About Economics."
- **Description:** First 2 lines = the hook + keyword (shows in search). Then a
  fuller summary, timestamps (chapters!), and links.
- **Chapters:** Add timestamps — they create clickable chapters and boost
  retention and search.
- **Tags:** Light touch; YouTube mostly uses title/thumbnail/description now.
- **Playlists:** Put every episode in a "Stardate Files" playlist so binge
  sessions chain automatically.
- **Shorts:** Cut a 45–60s hook from each long video as a Short to feed the
  algorithm new viewers.

## 6. Copyright & fair use (read this)

Star Trek is owned by **Paramount.** You can absolutely make Trek content, but:

- ✅ **Generally safer:** your original commentary/analysis, your own voice,
  visuals *you* create or generate, short transformative clips used for direct
  critique, royalty-free music.
- ⚠️ **Risky / can get struck or demonetized:** long unedited scenes, full
  episodes, playing show music in the background, using clips as filler rather
  than commentary.
- 🛡️ **Practical rule:** Lead with *your* analysis. Treat any show footage as a
  brief illustration of a point you're actively making — not as content itself.
- 🎵 **Music:** Use royalty-free / YouTube Audio Library / licensed (Epidemic
  Sound, Artlist). Never the actual Trek scores.
- 🏷️ **Branding:** Don't put "Star Trek" or Starfleet logos in your channel name
  or merch — build *your* brand (e.g., "Stardate Files") that's *about* Trek.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Fair use is fact-specific.

## 7. A sustainable weekly workflow

The secret to not quitting is **batching.** Don't do all steps for one video;
do one step for many videos.

**Monthly batch day 1 — Write:** Draft 4 scripts using the template.
**Monthly batch day 2 — Record:** Record all 4 voiceovers in one session (same
energy, same mic setup).
**Weekly — Edit & ship:** Edit one episode, make thumbnails, schedule it.

This way one bad week doesn't break your publishing streak — you've got a buffer.

### Per-episode timeline (rough)
1. Pick topic from `episode-ideas.md`
2. Write script from `script-template.md` (~2–3 hrs)
3. Record voiceover (~30–45 min for a 10-min video)
4. Gather/generate B-roll & visuals
5. Edit (~3–6 hrs starting out, faster later)
6. Thumbnail (3 variants) + title + description
7. Run through `publishing-checklist.md`
8. Schedule for your fixed publish day
