# Channel Strategy

## 1. Niche & positioning

Star Trek is a *huge* topic with a passionate, older-skewing, highly engaged
fanbase — which is great for watch time and comments, but means you need a
**sharp angle** to stand out from established channels (Trekspertise, Ex Astris
Scientia, Certifiably Ingame, etc.).

Don't be "a Star Trek channel." Be **one specific show**:

> **"The channel that [does ONE thing] about Star Trek."**

Strong, defensible angles for a new creator (pick ONE to lead with):

| Angle | Why it works | Example episode |
|-------|-------------|-----------------|
| **Lore deep-dives** | Evergreen, searchable, binge-able | "Why the Federation Has No Money — Explained" |
| **Ship/tech breakdowns** | Visual, fan-favorite, great thumbnails | "Every Starship Class, Ranked by Firepower" |
| **Episode rewatch/reviews** | Recurring, taps nostalgia + new shows | "Was 'The Inner Light' the Best TNG Episode?" |
| **Philosophy/ethics essays** | High-retention, shareable, low competition | "The Prime Directive Is a Lie" |
| **News & theories** | Rides trends/algorithm spikes | "Everything We Know About Starfleet Academy" |

**Recommendation:** Lead with **lore deep-dives** as your flagship "show," and
sprinkle in **news/theory** videos when there's a franchise moment (new trailer,
new series). Lore deep-dives are evergreen — they keep earning views for years,
which is how small channels compound.

## 2. Your flagship format: "Stardate Files"

A named, recurring series gives viewers a reason to subscribe and binge.

- **Format:** 8–14 min single-topic lore/explainer episode
- **Structure:** Cold-open hook → title card → 3–5 segments → payoff → CTA
- **Cadence:** 1 episode/week (batch-record monthly)
- **Identity:** Same intro sting, same title-card style, same sign-off line
  every episode ("Live long, and we'll see you next stardate.")

Rename it anything you like: *Starfleet Files, The Trek Files, Stardate
Briefing, Captain's Log,* etc.

## 3. Target audience

- **Primary:** 25–55, lifelong Trek fans + people rewatching on streaming
- **Secondary:** Newcomers who got into Trek via recent shows (Strange New
  Worlds, Discovery, Picard) and want lore context
- **What they want:** To feel smarter about something they love, in a tight,
  well-edited package — *not* a 45-min ramble

## 4. Channel name ideas

Pick something short, memorable, and not tied to one show so you can grow:

- **Stardate Files**
- **Warp Core** / **The Warp Core**
- **Subspace Signal**
- **Delta Quadrant** (if you want a Voyager-ish vibe but keep it broad)
- **LCARS Log**
- **The Ready Room Table** (note: "The Ready Room" is an official Paramount show — avoid that exact name)
- **Trek Decoded**
- **Bridge Officer**
- **Stardate** (clean, brandable)

> Check availability on YouTube **and** as a handle (@yourname) and ideally a
> matching .com before committing.

## 5. Growth plan (first 90 days)

**Goal:** Not subscribers — **watch time + a binge-able back catalog.** 30
solid evergreen videos beats 3 viral ones for a new channel.

- **Weeks 1–2:** Lock name/branding. Batch-record episodes 1–4. Publish #1.
- **Weeks 3–8:** Publish 1/week. Reply to *every* comment. Study which topics
  over-perform and make more of those.
- **Weeks 9–12:** Double down on your 2–3 best-performing topics. Make a
  "playlist series" so viewers auto-roll into the next episode.

**Levers that actually move the needle for new channels:**
1. **Thumbnail + title** — this is 80% of whether a video gets clicked. Make 3
   thumbnail options per video; we have an AI image tool wired in (see
   `tools-and-automation.md`).
2. **First 30 seconds** — most viewers leave here. The cold-open hook in the
   script template is designed to fight this.
3. **Packaging consistency** — same look so your videos are recognizable in the
   feed.
4. **Topic selection** — search-driven evergreen topics ("explained", "ranked",
   "why does X") get discovered for years.

## 6. Publishing cadence

| Phase | Frequency | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| Launch | 1/week, fixed day | Consistency > volume. Pick a day, never miss it. |
| If you have time | 2/week | Add a shorter "theory/news" video midweek |
| Always | 2–3 Shorts/week | Cut 45–60s hooks from your long videos for reach |

## 7. Metrics to watch (and ignore)

- **Watch first:** Click-through rate (CTR, aim >4%), average view duration /
  percentage viewed (aim >50%).
- **Watch second:** Returning viewers, subscribers-per-video.
- **Mostly ignore early:** Total subscriber count, single-video virality.

## 8. Monetization (later)

You need 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours (or 10M Shorts views) for YouTube
Partner Program. Don't optimize for money early. When you're ready:
- AdSense, channel memberships, Patreon, merch (careful with Trek IP — sell
  *your brand*, not Star Trek trademarks), affiliate links to Blu-rays/books.
